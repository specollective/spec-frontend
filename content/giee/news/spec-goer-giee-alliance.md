2026 marks a milestone for the Global Inclusive Education Ecosystem (GIEE): the Sustainable Progress and Equality Collective (SPEC) and the Global Online Education Regulator (GOER) are coming together as the Global Inclusive Education Ecosystem's (GIEE's) two primary partners, uniting around a shared mission to make AI in education inclusive, accountable, and human-centered. This work is part of a UN-affiliated Geneva Forum - Global Impact Project.

## Where This Started

The roots of this work trace back to the Geneva Forum, established in 2001 in partnership with Objectif Sciences International (OSI) and United Nations entities. For over two decades, the Forum has served as an incubator for initiatives advancing the UN Sustainable Development Goals through research, policy, and field action - connecting UN affiliates, impact finance, research centers, and the private sector to move ideas from concept to measurable implementation.

GIEE did not begin as a finished framework; it began as a question. Over the past several months, a small community of educators, technologists, and policy advocates worked through rounds of structuring, calibration, peer review, and field testing to figure out what responsible AI adoption in education could actually look like in practice, not just on paper. That work produced a portfolio of real case studies - from learner-agency pilots to AI literacy programs - each testing a different piece of what "trustworthy AI in education" means on the ground.

GIEE grew out of that ecosystem: an AI-driven, human-centric framework built to ensure equitable access to technology through community-driven research, real-world case studies, and human-in-the-loop development. It did not emerge from a single lab or boardroom - it was shaped by a village of collaborators. GIEE's branding and marketing leads - Joe Torreggiani, Oleg Shilin, Dennise Rodríguez Ávila, and Ilia de Leon - along with GIEE case study leads Dr. Krishauna Hines-Gaither, Myriam Da Silva, Mark Hooley, Dr. Nan Travers, Dr. Tarek Francis, and Tiera Tanksley have spent months turning that framework into practice, actively building out seven GIEE case studies spanning structural inclusion in AI (Women in AI), AI-enabled knowledge mapping (Insight Recognition), critical AI literacy for K-12 educators, clinician-supervised AI mental health support, movement-based learning, neuroscience-informed pathways for Special Educational Needs students, and purpose-driven approaches to youth screen dependency.

What that process made clear was that no single organization could carry this forward alone. Building genuine trust requires both a values-driven advocate for equity and access, and an operational regulator capable of turning principles into enforceable standards. That recognition is what has brought SPEC and GOER to the table together.

That momentum was recognized this year when GIEE leadership was named a Geneva Forum Leader in Project-Based Learning, alongside Jérémy Labouré, Thomas Egli, and the Objectif Sciences International team - a recognition that reflects just how central this work has become to the Forum's broader mission.

## Why Now

AI is entering classrooms faster than the guardrails meant to govern it. Personalization engines, automated assessment, and AI tutoring tools are already shaping how millions of learners engage with education - often without a clear architecture of trust behind them: no shared standard for data sovereignty, no consistent human-in-the-loop safeguards, no common bar for inclusion or bias mitigation. Every month without coordinated standards is a month where fragmented, inconsistent practices become the default. With the Road to Geneva 2027 and the Geneva AI Summit 2027 on the horizon, the window to shape shared, evidence-based standards - rather than react to their absence - is narrowing. That urgency is exactly why SPEC and GOER have chosen to move now, together.

## What Comes Next

Together with GIEE, SPEC and GOER will carry this collaboration into the 18th Geneva Forum this December and toward the Geneva AI Summit 2027 - turning field evidence from case studies already underway into standards that could shape how AI is governed in classrooms worldwide. Formal documentation will follow. The commitment already has.
